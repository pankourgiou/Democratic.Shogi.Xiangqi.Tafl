Democratic Shogi, Xiangqi & Tafl

Real rules, but nobody decides alone. On your turn seven advisors propose moves and you vote from their ballot (or issue an executive order). On its turn the council debates and the voting program you pick decides.

The council’s debate appears here on its turn.

Games

  • Shogi: 9×9, captured pieces can be dropped (not two unpromoted pawns in one file, no pawn-drop mate, no dropping where a piece has no move). Promotion in the far three rows is optional, and forced only when the piece would have no moves. You can’t leave your king in check. You are red (bottom). Win by checkmate.
  • Xiangqi: palace, river, elephant eyes, horse legs, cannons jump to capture; soldiers step sideways only after crossing the river, never backward, and the two generals may not face each other on an open file. You can’t leave your general in check. Win by checkmate or stalemate.
  • Tafl (Nordic chess / Hnefatafl, 11×11): you are the defenders with the king, the council attacks. Pieces move like rooks, captures are by sandwiching. Only the king may land on the throne or corners. The king escapes to a corner to win; attackers win by surrounding the king on four sides (three plus the throne), and a king on the edge cannot be captured.

The democratic layer

  • Advisors: Hawk attacks, Guardian protects, Surveyor wants mobility, Pioneer pushes progress, Treasurer guards material, Trickster is erratic, Pragmatist balances.
  • Your turn: vote for a ballot option (or click its lettered ring on the board).
  • Executive order (free mode): play any legal move with no ballot. You get 3 per game. The council has none.
  • Council turn: advisors propose, debate (they can change their minds), then the program decides.

Not implemented: repetition rules (sennichite, perpetual check or chase in xiangqi) and, in tafl, the Copenhagen shieldwall capture, edge-fort win and encirclement win. A side with no legal move loses.
